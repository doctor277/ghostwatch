## Alternatives considered

| Option | Practical effect | Portfolio fit | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIT | Permissive use, modification, and redistribution with copyright/license notice | Simple choice for broad viewing and reuse | No explicit patent grant beyond applicable law |
| Apache License 2.0 | Permissive terms plus an explicit patent license and contribution protections | Strong choice when patent language matters | Longer and more formal than MIT |
| GNU GPL v3 | Derivative distributions must provide corresponding source under GPL-compatible terms | Appropriate when downstream software freedom is the priority | Some commercial users avoid strong copyleft |
| GNU AGPL v3 | GPL-style obligations also apply when modified software is offered over a network | Strongest open-source sharing requirement listed here | Often unsuitable when broad commercial adoption is desired |
| No public license | Copyright remains reserved; viewers may inspect the public repository but do not receive general reuse rights | Suitable for code-showcase-only intent | Limits collaboration, reuse, and distribution clarity |

## Original decision guide

- Choose **MIT** for the simplest permissive portfolio release.
- Choose **Apache-2.0** for permissive reuse with an explicit patent grant.
- Choose **GPL-3.0** when redistributed derivatives should remain open source.
- Choose **AGPL-3.0** when network-hosted modifications should also remain open source.
- Choose **no public license** when the repository is only a visible portfolio sample and reuse is not authorized.

Before deciding, confirm that all bundled code and assets are owned by the publisher or compatible with the chosen terms. Package licenses and generated-image provenance should be reviewed as part of publication. This summary is practical guidance, not legal advice.
